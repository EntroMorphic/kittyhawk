# #8 Falsification probe — does BitNet's FFN tolerate slice masking?

Per `docs/TRIT_ROUTING_APPLICATIONS.md` item #8. Scope red-team first,
then minimum informative probe.

## Scope red-team

Full #8 (substrate routing as MoE gating mechanism) requires:
- A MoE harness (~1 week to build; doesn't exist).
- A trained learned-gate baseline (~1 week + real training compute).
- Substrate-routing implementation against that baseline (~1 week).
- Quality + load-balancing measurement (~1 week).

Honest scope: **3-5 weeks of work and infrastructure that isn't in
the project.** Cannot fit in this LMM cycle.

**Minimum informative probe** (this work-unit): partition BitNet's
existing FFN intermediate (6912 dims) into N=4 synthetic "slices" of
1728 dims each. Mask k of N slices per token; observe whether the
model tolerates this and whether smart selection (oracle ground-truth
score) beats random. This answers the **prerequisite question for #8**:
- If the FFN cannot tolerate slice masking at all → #8 premise dead
- If oracle ≈ random → no room for smart routing
- If oracle > random and both tolerate some sparsity → routing
  matters; substrate-routing would have room to add value

## Implementation

`gesh/bitnet/bitnet_harness.c`:
- `bitnet_ffn_mode_t` enum: `dense | oracle | random`.
- `BITNET_FFN_MODE`, `BITNET_FFN_NUM_EXPERTS` (default 4),
  `BITNET_FFN_K` (default 2) env vars.
- `bitnet_ffn_apply_slice_mask()`: scores each slice (oracle = sum
  |gate_act[slice]|; random = xorshift32 noise), picks top-k by
  signed descending score, zeros all positions in non-kept slices.
- Hook into FFN forward path: applied after `gate_act` computation,
  before `ffn_sub_norm`. Dense mode = no-op (default).
- Build clean, all 29 ctest binaries pass.

## Experiment: mini-focused subset (n=4)

4 prompts × 5 configs:
- `dense`
- `oracle k=2 N=4` (50% mask)
- `oracle k=3 N=4` (25% mask)
- `random k=2 N=4` (50% mask)
- `random k=3 N=4` (25% mask)

### Per-prompt qualitative read

| prompt | dense | oracle_k2 | oracle_k3 | random_k2 | random_k3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| math_div | "12<br>How many" | **LOOP** on " 12" | "what?<br>Answer:" | **LOOP** | partial loop |
| factual_capital | "Answer: Paris..." | "Answer: The capital of France is Paris. It is the most populous city in" | "Answer: ...Paris is the largest city in the" | **LOOP-Q** | drifts |
| code_loop | natural Python | **LOOP** | "# Iterate through the 10 numbers" | **LOOP** | partial loop |
| edge_single | "I am a new user..." | weird-but-coherent | coherent | **LOOP** | coherent (surprise) |

### Findings

1. **25% slice masking (oracle, k=3/N=4) is robust.** All 4 prompts
   produce coherent (different) text. The FFN tolerates losing 25% of
   slice contributions when the dropped slices are the lowest-contributing.

2. **50% slice masking (oracle, k=2/N=4) is task-sensitive.** Works on
   `factual_capital` and `edge_single`; breaks on `math_div` and
   `code_loop`. Math + code tasks need broader slice participation;
   factual + conversational tolerate more aggressive masking.

3. **Oracle > random consistently.** At both k=2 and k=3, random
   masking degrades more than oracle. This is the load-bearing finding
   for #8's premise: **smart slice selection MATTERS** — the FFN
   slices are not equivalent. There's room for a routing strategy to
   outperform random.

4. **Random k=3 has surprise coherence on edge_single.** "I'm writing
   this to you, because I know that you're a lot greater than me."
   At 25% mask, random is sometimes lucky. Not a contradiction;
   confirms that at moderate sparsity, the FFN has some slack.

## What this validates / does not validate

**Validates for #8:**
- BitNet's FFN tolerates 25% slice sparsity robustly.
- Smart slice selection beats random → routing strategies can pay off.
- Prerequisite question answered: substrate routing has a target it
  could improve.

**Does NOT validate:**
- Substrate routing as the gating mechanism (not implemented in this
  probe; oracle is the proxy).
- Quality at 50% sparsity (task-sensitive even with oracle).
- Anything about a trained MoE comparison.

## Why substrate-routed gating wasn't implemented here

The cheap probe used oracle scores (computed from full gate_act),
which doesn't save compute. Substrate-routed gating would route on
x_norm signature vs precomputed slice characteristic signatures,
which:
- Requires offline precomputation of per-slice characteristic sigs
  from gate_proj weight column-sums (~half day implementation).
- The characteristic-signature design is itself a research question:
  column-mean? max-magnitude direction? Multiple candidates worth
  testing.
- Without a trained MoE baseline to compare against, the value of
  substrate routing on this *un-trained* synthetic-slice setup is
  unclear: would substrate routing even match oracle here?

These belong in a follow-up cycle with proper scoping. The current
probe accomplishes its purpose: identifies the operating regime and
confirms there's room for routing to matter.

## Red-team

### C1: Synthetic slices ≠ real MoE experts
Real MoE experts are *trained* to specialize; BitNet's FFN slices are
not. The probe results bound expectations for THIS model, not for a
trained MoE. A future MoE-style model could behave very differently
at 50% sparsity. So the "FFN tolerates X% sparsity" claim is
narrowly scoped to BitNet b1.58-2B-4T.

### C2: n=4 is small
Mini-focused subset for cheap signal. The pattern (oracle > random,
25% tolerable, 50% task-sensitive) is consistent across 4 diverse
prompts, but full 10-prompt focused-subset measurement would tighten
the claim. Recorded as follow-up; not blocking.

### C3: Oracle score is a proxy
Oracle scores each slice by `sum |gate_act[slice]|` — magnitude-only.
This is one possible "ground truth" for slice importance; an
alternative is sum of `gate_act[slice]² × up[slice]² /
post_norm_contribution`, which more accurately reflects each slice's
contribution to the next layer. Using a different oracle might shift
the 25%-vs-50% boundary, but unlikely to flip the oracle-vs-random
ordering.

### C4: K-of-N substrate routing has unsolved design choices
For substrate routing to be implemented:
- Where does the characteristic signature come from? (Offline weight
  aggregation? Running statistic during inference?)
- What tau is used for the slice characteristic signature?
- What about load balancing (substrate routing might cluster tokens
  to a few slices)?
These are open questions; this probe doesn't address them.

### C5: Discrete top-k is non-differentiable
For the substrate-routed gating to be trainable (in a future MoE
setting), the top-k decision needs a gradient estimator. Existing
MoE work uses Gumbel-softmax or straight-through estimators; the
substrate's popcount-distance metric integrates differently. Open
research question; outside this probe.

## Verdict

**#8's premise is alive at moderate sparsity (25%) on BitNet's FFN.**
The probe confirms (1) FFN tolerates slice masking at this level, and
(2) smart selection beats random — so substrate-routing has room to
add value. Full #8 implementation (substrate-routed gating against a
learned-gate baseline on a trained MoE model) is deferred to a
properly-scoped future cycle.

TIER-M to TIER-M-PROBE-DONE-FULL-DEFERRED, with the operating-regime
finding recorded.
