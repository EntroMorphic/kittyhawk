## Crucial methodological pivot

When dense itself failed (looped) on all three long-context prompts,
the initial instinct was to find "easier" prompts where dense
remained coherent. The user corrected this:

> "If Dense fails, that IS the signal. Proceed with the same
> long-context prompt."

The interesting question at long context is not "does eviction match
dense" but "does any eviction policy disrupt dense's loop attractor
in a useful way." This is the regime where KV eviction would
actually matter in production.

3 prompts × (1 dense + 1 fifo + 3 random seeds + 2 sigdist M ∈ {1, 8})

### Key data points

**`long_recall_alma` (factual recall — director name):**
- dense: " 2010-0-0-0-0..." → score 0
- sigdist_m1: " The answer is the Pacific's biology and the
  Pacific's biology..." → score 1 (loops but mentions "biology",
  which is relevant)

**`long_narrative_lighthouse` (story continuation):**
- dense: " the lighthouse. The storm was a storm. The lighthouse
  was a storm. The storm. The lighthouse" → score 1
- **sigdist_m1**: " the eleventh of the eleventh, the twelfth, the
  twelfth, the thirteenth, the fourteenth, the fifteenth" → **score 2**

The sigdist_m1 output **increments through dates** starting from the
prompt's "the eleventh of November." The model didn't get the
narrative right but found a different attractor — date enumeration —
that's structurally novel and grammatically clean. None of the other
configs do this.

**`long_recipe_extended` (factual recall — sour tapioca):**
- dense: " The answer is the answer is the answer is..." → score 0
- random_s1: " tapioca starch, tapioca starch, tapioca starch..."
  → score 1 (mentions the right ingredient!)
- **sigdist_m1**: " What is the type of tapioca starch is the type
  of tapioca starch is the type of tapioca starch is the type of
  tapioca starch" → **score 2**

sigdist_m1 produces "What is the type of tapioca starch" — the
**actual question structure** from the prompt. None of the other
configs reconstruct the question form.

## What was load-bearing in the correction

1. **Long-context test material.** Without it, the test regime
   doesn't match what eviction is designed for. At small seq_k,
   "should we evict?" is a near-no-op question.
2. **Quality rubric instead of dense-agreement.** When dense itself
   is broken at long context, agreement-with-dense rewards being
   ALSO broken. The rubric measures coherence directly.
3. **n=3 multi-seed for random.** Random's variance is ~0 at this
   sample (all three seeds rated 2/12). With single-seed, random
   could have looked spuriously good or bad; multi-seed shows the
   variance is low and the comparison is fair.
4. **Reading the outputs.** The lighthouse-date-incrementing and
   recipe-question-reconstruction findings would have been
   completely invisible at any aggregate-metric level. Spot-check
   discipline is non-negotiable for verdicts.

## sigdist_m1 vs sigdist_m8 — surprise reversal

- **Smoke test on long_history (small seq_k=66)**: M=8 looked better
  ("the most important invention of the 15th").
- **This rerun (seq_k 164-208)**: M=1 scores higher than M=8.

At small seq_k, M=8 averaging stays close to current state. At
larger seq_k, M=8 averages across more positions whose directions
have diverged, producing a more-blurred "direction proxy" that
behaves like fifo (3/12 on this rerun, matching M=8). M=1's sharper
direction signal preserves the anchoring effect.

**This is consistent with the diversity-collapse mechanism from the
original probe** — at small seq_k aggressive eviction, M=1 collapses
into loops; at moderate eviction with long enough seq_k, M=1's
sharp direction preserves prompt-relevant anchors. The dynamic
depends on how much "headroom" eviction has.

## Caveats — what this rerun does NOT establish

- **n=3 prompts.** The pattern is consistent across 3 diverse
  prompts (factual recall, narrative, factual recall) but n is
  small. A broader battery would tighten the claim.
- **One window (64).** Other windows (32 = more aggressive; 128 =
  more lenient) untested in the rerun.
- **One quality evaluator (self).** Per the rubric's protocol
  caveats, self-evaluation introduces internal-consistency bias.
- **Best-attractor argument.** The "sigdist disrupts dense's loop"
  story is mechanistic plausibility from n=3 observations. Could
  be that sigdist *also* fails in different ways on prompts I
  didn't test.

## Red-team

### C1: Did I cherry-pick the rubric scores?
Scoring was done by reading the decoded text and applying the
rubric defined BEFORE running the experiment. Scores:
- dense lighthouse: "the lighthouse. The storm was a storm. The
  lighthouse was a storm." → has SOME structure (grammar holds in
  fragments) → 1 not 0. Defensible.
- sigdist_m1 lighthouse: incrementing dates → grammatically clean,
  off-topic but structurally novel → 2 (coherent off-topic). Higher
  scores would require ON-TOPIC, which date-iteration isn't quite.
- sigdist_m1 recipe: "What is the type of tapioca starch is the
  type of tapioca starch" → forms the right question but loops → 2
  (between 1=partial-loop and 3=coherent-on-topic).

The scoring is conservative on sigdist where alternatives exist.
The 5-vs-2 gap (sigdist vs random) survives several reasonable
re-scorings.

### C2: Multi-seed random has near-zero variance — is that suspicious?
All 3 random seeds rated 2/12. This is plausible because random's
failure mode is "uniformly distributed eviction → loss of structural
anchors → generic loop." The failure is robust across seeds. If
anything, this STRENGTHENS the comparison: random doesn't get
"lucky" on any seed; sigdist_m1 doesn't compete against an unlucky
random run.

### C3: Why does fifo (3/12) match sigdist_m8 (3/12)?
fifo evicts oldest first; preserves recent context. sigdist_m8
averages over 8 positions, producing a blurred direction that may
behave similarly to "preserve recent." At long context, both score
similarly under the rubric. Plausible.

### C4: The lighthouse date-incrementing is one data point
True. But it's a categorically different failure mode from "loop on
same phrase." Even one categorical distinction (M=1 finds a NOVEL
attractor while alternatives find a generic loop) is meaningful for
the mechanism story. n=1 for the SPECIFIC date-increment pattern,
but n=3 for the broader "sigdist_m1 anchors to prompt-specific
content" claim (alma's "biology", lighthouse's dates, recipe's
question).

### C5: Could long_recipe's sigdist_m1 score 1 instead of 2?
"What is the type of tapioca starch is the type of tapioca starch"
reproduces the prompt's question form. That's grammatical English,
on-topic in the sense that the question is from the prompt, but
doesn't ANSWER. Score 2 (coherent off-topic from the desired-answer
perspective; coherent on-topic from the question-form perspective).
A stricter rater might give 1; a more lenient might give 3.
Sensitivity check: if I downgrade recipe sigdist_m1 to 1, total
becomes 4 instead of 5. Still beats random (2). Verdict holds.

## What's still deferred

The original red-team identified 5 more refinements that were not
implemented in this rerun:
- Q-sig direction (use Q vectors instead of K).
- Exclude N-most-recent from eviction candidates.
- Per-kv_head eviction (not position-level).
- Even longer context (seq_k > 1024 — what production really cares
  about).
- Broader prompt battery (n > 3).

These remain in the open-follow-ups list. The substrate eviction
concept has now earned a positive intermediate verdict; future work
should sharpen, not re-derive.

## Verdict — POSITIVE (with explicit caveats)

**The substrate-routed eviction concept (sigdist with current-K
direction proxy) is positively validated on long-context prompts

ingredient names, biology terms).

This corrects the original NEGATIVE verdict and the same-day MIXED
