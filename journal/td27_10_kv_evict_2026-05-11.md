# #10 KV cache eviction via signature distance — clean negative for the simple policy

Per `docs/TRIT_ROUTING_APPLICATIONS.md` item #10. Builds on #1 (K-sig
cache). Tests whether substrate signature-distance is a better KV
eviction heuristic than FIFO or random.

## Implementation

`gesh/bitnet/bitnet_config.h`: added `evicted` bitmask (per layer,
per position) to `bitnet_kv_cache_t`. Lazy-allocated.

`gesh/bitnet/bitnet_kv_cache.c`: alloc/free hooks for `evicted`.

`gesh/bitnet/bitnet_harness.c`:
- `bitnet_kv_evict_mode_t` enum: `none | fifo | random | sigdist`.
- `BITNET_KV_EVICT_MODE` + `BITNET_KV_WINDOW` env vars.
- `bitnet_kv_evict_pick_victim()` per policy:
  - **fifo**: oldest non-evicted position.
  - **random**: uniformly-random non-evicted (xorshift32).
  - **sigdist**: position with MAX summed-popcount K-sig distance
    to the current position's K-sig. Uses **current K-sig as the
    direction proxy** for "where the model is going now."
- `bitnet_kv_evict_apply()` triggered after each K-write + sig store.
- Attention path: dense mode masks evicted positions to
  `-M4T_MTFP_MAX_VAL` before softmax → softmax weight ≈ 0.
- sigdist mode auto-enables K-sig cache with default tau=5000 (per
  the #4 finding that fixed tau is acceptable).

## Sanity: window >> seq_k = bit-exact dense

math_div, gen 8 (max seq_k=15), `BITNET_KV_WINDOW=1000`. All three
policies produce identical output to dense:
```
dense:        220 717 198 633 14924 25 2650 1690
fifo_w1000:   220 717 198 633 14924 25 2650 1690
random_w1000: 220 717 198 633 14924 25 2650 1690
sigdist_w1000:220 717 198 633 14924 25 2650 1690
```
Implementation correctness verified.

## Experiment: 4-prompt focused subset × 7 configs

Prompts: `long_history`, `long_summary`, `nar_storm`, `code_comment`.
All have max seq_k ≥ 60 so window=16 (~75% eviction) and window=32
(~50% eviction) both exercise the eviction path. Each prompt run with
`dense / fifo_w{16,32} / random_w{16,32} / sigdist_w{16,32}`.

### First-30-token agreement with dense (aggregate over 4 prompts; max 120)

| policy        | w=16 | w=32 |
|---------------|------|------|
| fifo          | 20   | 70   |
| random        | **28** | **78** |
| sigdist       | 26   | 60   |

**At both windows, random ≥ sigdist.** The simple sigdist policy does
NOT beat random eviction on this battery.

### Loop heuristic (8-gram repeats ≥ 3 = LOOP)

Only one config triggered the loop heuristic:
- **nar_storm sigdist_w16: LOOP**.

Inspecting the actual text:
```
The only sound was the patter of the rain. The rain was the
only sound. The rain was the only sound. The rain was the only
sound. The rain ...
```

This is the **self-reinforcing diversity collapse** failure mode.
At each token, the model is in a "the rain" state; its current K-sig
is close to recent K-sigs; sigdist evicts the *diverse* older
positions (which are most-distant from current direction); the model
loses semantic anchors; the loop self-reinforces.

fifo and random on the same prompt produce coherent narrative
continuations without loops.

### Per-prompt qualitative read (window=32)

| prompt | dense | fifo_w32 | random_w32 | sigdist_w32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| long_history | "books were copied by hand, which was a very slow process..." | "books were written by hand and were very tedious..." | "books were copied by hand, which was a slow and tedious process..." | "the spread of knowledge was limited to the monks and scholars..." |
| long_summary | itself loops on "Roman Empire was complex..." | coherent novel | coherent novel | **structural loop** "which in turn... in the other... which in turn" |
| nar_storm | partial loop on "thunder/lightning" | coherent first-person narrative | coherent descriptive | coherent description |
| code_comment | normal | normal | normal | normal |

At w=32, sigdist is sometimes-coherent, sometimes structurally
broken. At w=16, sigdist explicitly collapses on at least one prompt.

## Verdict

**Negative result for sigdist with "current K-sig as direction proxy."**

The hypothesis was: direction-aware eviction (substrate-distinctive)
beats direction-blind eviction (fifo, random). The data shows:

1. **sigdist does not beat random** on dense-agreement at either
   window (60/120 vs 78/120 at w=32; 26/120 vs 28/120 at w=16).
2. **sigdist has a diversity-collapse failure mode** (nar_storm
   sigdist_w16 loops; long_summary sigdist_w32 structural loop).
3. **Random eviction is surprisingly competitive** — even fifo
   loses to random on some prompts.

The substrate-distinctive premise of #10 is **NOT validated** at this
implementation level. The eviction decision needs a smarter direction
proxy than "current K-sig" for sigdist to deliver on its premise.

## Why the simple sigdist fails — mechanism

When the model is in a repeating state (e.g., "The rain"):
- Each new token's K-sig is close to recent K-sigs (similar direction).
- sigdist picks "MAX distance" victims → evicts the diverse older
  positions.
- Diverse anchors disappear from the cache.
- The model has nothing pulling it away from the repeating state.
- Self-reinforcing loop.

Random eviction, by contrast, evicts uniformly — diverse positions
survive with probability proportional to their count, preserving
escape paths from local attractors.

## Red-team

### C1: 4 prompts is small
Aggregate (4 prompts × 3 policies × 2 windows = 24 datapoints) is
limited. The negative-result signal is consistent across prompts on
the failure mode but absolute pass-rate ordering might shift with
more prompts.

### C2: First-30-token agreement isn't quality
As discussed in #3 red-team, agreement with dense is divergence, not
quality. Several "low-agreement" outputs are coherent alternative
continuations. The LOOP heuristic catches the real failure (nar_storm
sigdist_w16) more reliably than agreement.

### C3: Other direction proxies untested
The probe used current K-sig as direction. Other choices:
- **Running mean of recent K-sigs** (over last M tokens) — smoother
  direction; might resist diversity collapse.
- **Running mean of recent Q-sigs** — captures the *query* direction
  rather than what's *stored*; aligns with attention's actual decision.
- **Exclude N most-recent positions from eviction candidates** —
  preserves a "recency window" alongside the substrate decision.

Any of these might lift sigdist above random. This probe doesn't
test them.

### C4: Position-level eviction may be wrong granularity
sigdist evicts a single position across all 5 kv_heads at once. The
"max distance" decision is a sum over kv_heads. But different
kv_heads might judge different positions as valuable. Per-kv_head
eviction (one position evicted per kv_head independently) would be
more accurate but more memory.

### C5: No long-context test
Max seq_k in this battery is ~71. The regime where KV eviction
actually matters (e.g., seq_k > 1024 with cache pressure) is
unmeasured. The probe answers feasibility + smart-routing question
at moderate scale, not the production-scale value of the eviction.

## What this DOES validate

- **The eviction infrastructure works**: bit-exact dense at large
  window; coherent (mostly) at small window.
- **Eviction is tolerable** in BitNet b1.58-2B-4T at 50% (w=32 on
  ~66 seq_k) for non-substrate policies.
- **The substrate's K-sig cache (from #1) composes cleanly** with
  the new eviction policy infrastructure.

## What this does NOT validate

- **sigdist as a substrate-distinctive win.** Not validated at this
  implementation level.
- **Long-context eviction quality.** Unmeasured.
- **Other substrate eviction policies** (running-mean direction,
  Q-sig direction, per-kv_head). Untested.

## Open follow-ups (recorded; not pursuing in this cycle)

- Re-implement sigdist with running-mean K-sig direction; rerun.
- Try Q-sig direction (requires capturing Q-sig per attention step).
- Long-context battery (seq_k > 1024) once long-context test
  material exists.
- Compare against an attention-weight-based eviction (evict positions
  with consistently-low attention weight across recent steps) — that
  would be the strongest non-substrate baseline.

## Verdict — closed as NEGATIVE

**#10 status: implementation done; substrate-routed eviction premise
NOT validated by this probe.** The naive sigdist policy doesn't beat
random eviction and has a clean failure mode (diversity collapse).
The infrastructure stays in the codebase (env-gated, off by default)
in case future work wants to refine the policy.

Documented in `docs/TRIT_ROUTING_APPLICATIONS.md` item #10 with
NEGATIVE-RESULT status.

This is the substrate's second clean negative (after P0-4). Honestly
recorded.

## AMENDMENT 2026-05-11 — red-team caught overclaim, downgrading to MIXED RESULT

The original NEGATIVE verdict above is **too strong**. Red-teamed
later the same day; the following methodology gaps were identified
and are now recorded here:

### M1. Used a metric I had already disclaimed
In `#3 (hybrid)`'s red-team section I wrote: *"first-30-token
agreement with dense is divergence, not quality. Two arms can both
be coherent yet diverge from dense in different directions."* Then
I used exactly that metric to declare #10 a clean negative, without
spot-checking the "low agreement" cases.

When I went back and spot-checked the outputs:
- long_history sigdist_w32: *"the spread of knowledge was limited
  to the monks and scholars who had access to books and manuscripts.
  The invention of the printing press in the 15th..."* — **coherent,
  factual, on-topic.** Different from dense, not worse.
- long_summary sigdist_w32 *does* show structural pattern looping
  ("which in turn, in the other...") — that part of the original
  verdict survives.

The 78-vs-60 agreement gap (random vs sigdist) at w=32 conflates
"different valid continuation" with "lower quality" on at least
some prompts. The original analysis didn't catch this.

### M2. The smoke test showed sigdist preserving topic — I noted then forgot
On long_history at w=16, sigdist produced "the printing press was
the main feature of the inventions of books and the spread of
knowledge" before degenerating. fifo drifted entirely off-topic
("everyone can read and write"); random was generic. The smoke test
flagged sigdist as **anchoring to the prompt's actual subject**.
The aggregate metric erased that intermediate signal; the final
verdict didn't account for it.

### M3. Diversity collapse is n=1 across configs, not a general failure
The nar_storm sigdist_w16 loop ("The rain was the only sound") is a
real specific failure with a plausible mechanism. But it's **one
prompt out of four at the most aggressive window**. Reporting it as
"sigdist has a diversity-collapse failure mode" conflates one
demonstrated failure with a general claim about the policy.

### M4. random uses one seed; its variance is unmeasured
The 78/120 first-30-agreement for random_w32 came from a single
xorshift seed (0xC0FFEE10). Random's seed-to-seed variance is itself
a property of the policy. Without multi-seed measurement, treating
random's aggregate as a stable baseline is unsupported.

### M5. One direction proxy tested, labeled the concept negative
The probe used **current K-sig as direction**. The docs had
originally proposed **running mean Q-sig**. I changed the design
for implementation simplicity and never reverted. Saying "sigdist
doesn't work" overclaims when only the simplest implementation was
tested.

### M6. Bounded to small seq_k
All prompts max seq_k ≤ 71. KV eviction is for long contexts
(seq_k >> window in production). At seq_k=4096 with window=128,
the eviction regime is qualitatively different — the candidate
pool is large and "evict most-distant" might behave very
differently than at seq_k=66 with window=16. The negative result
is bounded to the small-seq_k regime tested.

### M7. Same class of error as the #5 spot-check lesson
In #5 (posracle full battery), a heuristic flagged sigdist-like
"wins" that turned out to be heuristic FPs once outputs were
spot-checked. The discipline I named there ("spot-check
heuristic-flagged disagreements before publishing claims") didn't
generalize automatically to #10. I spot-checked the LOOP case
(confirmed real) but not the "low agreement, presumably worse"
cases — where actual coherent on-topic English was being
mislabeled as "worse than random."

## Corrected verdict — MIXED RESULT

**sigdist with current-K-sig direction proxy is competitive but
not decisively better than random** at moderate eviction (w=32,
50% eviction). It **has a clean failure mode (diversity collapse)
demonstrated on ONE prompt at aggressive eviction** (w=16, 75%
eviction). At moderate eviction it **sometimes preserves prompt-
anchored topic** in ways the dense-agreement metric obscures. **The
substrate-routed eviction concept is NOT cleanly falsified.**
Refinements (running-mean direction, Q-sig direction, exclude-N-
most-recent, multi-seed random comparison, long-context regime)
could shift the picture; not tested.

The infrastructure stays in the codebase. The diversity-collapse
mechanism is a real concern for any "evict by direction" policy
and should be considered when designing refinements.

## What the original NEGATIVE verdict was meant to capture (and got right)

- The simplest substrate-routed eviction policy doesn't trivially
  beat random.
- There is at least one demonstrated failure mode (nar_storm sigdist_w16).
- No "clear win for substrate routing" claim is supportable from
  this probe.

## What the original NEGATIVE verdict OVERCLAIMED

- That sigdist is "the substrate's second clean negative." (It is
  not. The diversity collapse is real but the broader concept is
  not falsified.)
- That random "consistently beats sigdist." (At one seed it does on
  aggregate dense-agreement; at the qualitative read it's mixed.)
- That the result generalizes to long-context production regime.
  (It doesn't — bounded to seq_k ≤ 71.)

Cross-references P0-4 (the actual second clean negative): P0-4
had pre-registered success criteria, ran across multiple benchmarks
and variants, and produced a falsifying result against a clearly-
stated hypothesis. #10 had none of that rigor; calling it the
"second clean negative" conflated honesty with rigor.
